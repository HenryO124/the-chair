# How to make a thousand questions instead of a hundred

A maker who searches for one fact at a time spends a search and two fetches per
question, and the search budget runs out at about a hundred. A maker who fetches
**one list and turns it into twenty questions** spends one search and one fetch
for twenty. That is the whole difference, and it is the only way the numbers the
owner wants are reachable from here.

## The method

1. Take the next topic from the list below.
2. Find its source page with **one** search, fetch it **once**.
3. Read the whole table or list, and write **every question it supports** before
   moving on — typically 10 to 30.
4. The wrong answers come from the same list. That is why this works: a list of
   Finnish lakes by area gives you the right answer *and* three plausible wrong
   ones, already comparable, already sourced, with no second lookup.
5. Record the source URL once for the whole batch of questions from that page.
6. Only then take the next topic.

## What makes a good source page

A table or a list where the rows are of one kind and the columns hold the facts:
lakes by area, kings in order, elements by group, films by year, winners by
year, species in a family, capitals, bridges by span, islands by size.

Avoid prose articles. One article about one person yields one question and costs
the same as a table that yields thirty.

## What still has to be checked

The list is the source, so the fact is sourced — but two things are not:

- **The wrong answers must be wrong.** A list of Nordic capitals makes Oslo a
  fine wrong answer for a question about Sweden. A list of "cities sometimes
  considered Nordic" does not. Read what the list says it is.
- **The list must not move.** Winners before a dated year are safe; current
  holders, league tables and populations to the digit are not.

## A hundred and twenty lines

Each line is 10 to 30 questions. Twenty lines a category, six categories. If
every line yields fifteen, that is **1800 questions from about 120 searches** —
well inside one maker's budget, let alone six.
